## 6. Research Narrative

My research path started in computer vision and optimization. During my undergraduate research, I worked with particle swarm optimization and extreme learning machines for time-series prediction. Later, I moved into computer vision, object detection, semantic segmentation, and domain adaptation.

My bachelor’s thesis and early publication focused on cross-domain car detection, especially adapting models from daytime to nighttime imagery using unsupervised image-to-image translation. During my master’s degree, I continued this direction and worked on unsupervised domain adaptation for object detection using synthetic target images, CycleGAN, AdaIN, and neural style transfer.

This research gave me a strong interest in generalization: how models behave when the deployment distribution differs from training data, and how representations can become more robust and transferable.

Today, I want to extend this direction toward modern representation learning, vision-language models, multimodal models, world models, and embodied AI. I am especially interested in how models learn useful internal representations of the world, how they generalize beyond their training distribution, and how they can be evaluated or improved in more realistic settings.
